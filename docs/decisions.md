# Decisions

One line per decision, newest last. This file records what changed the
project's direction.

## Budgets

- Initial JS: under 600 KB gzipped.
- The scene route: under 1 MB gzipped first load.
- No single static asset over 1 MB.
- Check by reading `next build` output until it matters enough to automate.
- 2026-09-18 measured: `next build` on Next 16 prints no size table; measure first-load JS in browser devtools (Network, JS, gzipped) when it matters.

## Budgets (amended 2026-09-22)

- One hero model (the intro avatar) may be up to 3 MB, lazy-loaded after first paint, never in first paint.
- 2026-09-23: intro room immersive: the room is fully dressed (walls, ceiling, door, window bay with instanced blinds and a rain-on-glass shader over a canvas skyline, shelf wall with canvas book spines, record player, cassette deck, photo, neon in the script font, cork board, floor cushion, string lights, a cat), alive (dust in the lamp cone, steam, twinkle, keys that press with the typing, phone pulses, record and fan turn, filament flicker, camera breathing, pointer parallax, head look, one establishing dolly per session) and interactive (lamp, record player/sound, blinds cord, cat, notes, mug, poster; the same toggles as keyboard-reachable buttons in the overlay; a tooltip names what the pointer is over). Zero new assets: every texture is a canvas painter in lib/roomTextures.ts and all sound is Web Audio (lib/roomAudio.ts schedule), built on the first gesture. Detail mounts in two idle slices after first paint; two shadow maps on high, none below; instanced meshes are laid out per frame (a one-off effect lost its matrices). Book titles, notes and the neon phrase are placeholders (owner input).
- 2026-09-23: intro room re-dressed: open, nature-focused and modern in a soft earthy palette (plaster walls, pale oak, linen, sage, terracotta), golden-hour daylight (a sun through the glass with one 1024 shadow map, a sky hemisphere) instead of night; the back wall is three floor-to-ceiling glass panels with a painted garden and instanced trees outside for parallax; linen curtains on a rail replace the blinds (store key `curtainsOpen`); the neon is gone; plants roughly tripled (hanging pothos, plant stand, a bench of pots under the glass, two tall trunked plants, shelf and desk pots). The intro palette is now a warm cream so the overlay reads in ink, in a cartouche over the garden.
- 2026-09-23: baked room: the intro's static shell and furniture are built and lit in Blender (scripts/room/build.py: same coordinates as the code, Poly Haven CC0 PBR maps and HDRI, a second UV channel packed across all objects, sun + sky baked with Cycles on the GPU into one 2k lightmap carried as the glTF occlusion texture so TEXCOORD_1 survives export) and packed by scripts/room/pack.mjs (Draco, WebP, 1k maps) to public/models/room.glb (~1.4 MB). It loads after first paint and fades in over the procedural room, which hides its duplicates; the real-time sun and sky drop to half so dynamic things stay lit and shadowed without doubling the room. Fabrics and plaster use flat earthy colours with the scans' relief only. Budget exception: the room GLB up to 3 MB, lazy (scanned props included). Everything dynamic (screen, lamp, curtains, plants, keys, cat, avatar) stays real time.
- 2026-09-23: scanned props: Poly Haven CC0 models (potted_plant_01, potted_plant_02, dining_chair_02, desk_lamp_arm_01) fetched at 1k by scripts/room/fetchProps.mjs into assets/room/props (gitignored) and placed by its manifest.json (committed); the bake imports them, flattens them to world-space meshes, decimates anything over 40k triangles, gives them the lightmap channel and bakes them with the room; pack halves their textures to 512. The procedural chair, lamp body and the two floor plants hide when the baked room is in. Not on Poly Haven: a monitor, keyboard, headphones, cat, turntable; those stay procedural.
- 2026-09-23: eisen network (skeleton): chapter II keeps the screen framing and its hero is the software factory as a live orchestration network (core, three agent tier rings with per-container lifecycles, packets, a compliance ring of stamping gates, a substrate grid with pillars), drawn raw and bloomed, solid mode only. Eisen gains a three-beat plate, one beat per bullet, each anchored on a layer; `PlateBeat.lat` lets a non-globe plate set camera elevation. Beat longitudes are chosen so each anchor faces the camera (a local point at xz-angle α faces the camera at lon −α).
- 2026-09-23: eisen network is an orchestrator-worker network, not a hash ring: five meshed orchestrators each own a sector of seven worker containers; a worker cycle is spin up → task down → verdict (deterministic pass/fail from a seeded hash) → result up → tear down → gap, and a failed result flashes its orchestrator red before the slot respawns. Beat ii is now the verdicts ("Pass or fail"); the compliance ring and tier rings are gone.
- 2026-09-23: eisen factory phase 1 (spec superpowers/specs/2026-09-23-eisen-factory-design.md, local): the network is scaled 1.15 and its orchestrator ring widened (0.55) so the factory fills its band region and the plate frame; a `near` zoom step (k 2.6) sits between full and mid because mid put the near orchestrator across a third of the frame. Beats: i near at 42°, ii near at 10°, iii full at 38°. Phases 2 (terminal cards) and 3 (dive into the Meta page) are specified there.
- 2026-09-23: eisen factory phase 2: every worker is a terminal card. One canvas atlas holds twelve task columns (DEV/TEST/FEAT, placeholder names), two verdict strips and nine orchestrator status strips; a basic material patched with per-instance `aOrigin`/`aState` attributes windows eight rows of the atlas per screen (unprinted rows blank, verdict strip over the bottom row), so 35 screens stay one draw call. Strips sit on a two-line stride so mipmaps do not bleed neighbours. Orchestrator status labels are cutouts (dark ground discarded) and kept under the bloom threshold: in the headless capture a boxed label bloomed into a speckled band. Orchestrators turn to face their last dispatch; an eye marks the facing.
- 2026-09-23: eisen factory phase 3: a plate may end with `exit: "dive"`. It gains one scroll slot past its beats (no caption, no card), never shrinks back (`expandAmount(p, dive)`), and holds its hero to the end (`heroContinuous`). The instrument publishes the target screen to `plateState.dive` (centre, normal, half extents, world space); over the slot the camera blends from its orbit to the screen at the distance that covers the viewport in both axes, overfilled 10% because the smoothed camera trails the goal. Leaving a dive plate for another chapter is a cut: the camera, hero expansion, the old hero's weight and the engraving's paper and ink snap that frame. The page is a region of the terminal atlas painted in the next chapter's paper and codex face. `scripts/shots.mjs` reads `data-dive` and adds two fly-in frames.
- 2026-09-23: the lab is gone. The direction is committed: one scene at `/` — the studio room, the dive into Eisen's factory, the Meta globe. `lab/instruments` moved to `scene/`; the five other experiments (hello-cube, scroll-path, lighting-bench, chapters, studio-room), the registry, the loaders, the `/lab` routes and `docs/skeleton.md` are deleted, along with what only they used (`components/ScrollTrack`, `hooks/useScrollProgress`, `hooks/useInvalidateOnScroll`, `lib/roomMotion`, the chapters CSS block).
